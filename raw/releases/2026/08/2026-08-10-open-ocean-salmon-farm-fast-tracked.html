Open ocean salmon farm Fast-tracked

  • Hon Chris Bishop
  • Hon Shane Jones
Infrastructure
Oceans and Fisheries

The Fast-track approval of the Hananui Aquaculture Project off Rakiura/Stewart Island will bring significant benefits to Southland and New Zealand’s economy, Minister for Infrastructure Chris Bishop and Minister for Oceans and Fisheries Shane Jones say.

This is the 31st project and the first aquaculture project to be approved through the Fast-track process.

Ngāi Tahu Seafood Resources Limited lodged its application in November 2025, with approval taking around five months following the appointment of an independent expert panel.

“The Hananui project has taken around seven years to progress. Ngāi Tahu applied for consent under the RMA in 2019, which was later withdrawn. They then reapplied through the COVID-19 Fast-track process in 2020. Subsequently, in August 2023, the application was declined,” says Mr Bishop.

“Alongside this approval, our planning reforms will further support Māori commercial aquaculture, including by making it easier for iwi to unlock the potential of Aquaculture Settlement Areas provided for under the Māori Commercial Aquaculture Claims Settlement Act 2004, making the process easier, faster and more efficient.”

Mr Jones says that once Hananui is fully operational, it is projected to produce 14,400 tonnes of salmon per year, generating more than $310 million in sales, and creating 415 to 480 jobs in the region.

“This is almost as much salmon as was produced by all of New Zealand’s salmon farms in 2025-2026,” Mr Jones says.

“The global appetite for our king salmon is extremely high, representing a massive opportunity for New Zealand. To take full advantage of it we need to increase supply to meet this demand, and the approval of Hananui is an incredibly positive step in this direction.

“This Government has made aquaculture growth a focus, setting a $3 billion annual revenue target for the sector by 2035. A key pillar to achieving this goal identified in our Aquaculture Development Plan is large-scale open-ocean farming such as Hananui.

“Fast-track approval means these projects can proceed sooner, supporting jobs, regional growth, and export earnings, as well as New Zealand's position as a leader in sustainable seafood production.”

Fast-track by the numbers:

  • 31 projects approved by expert panels.
  • 16 projects with expert panels appointed.
  • 58 projects are currently progressing through the Fast-track process. 28 active substantive and 30 referral applications.
  • 65 projects have been referred to Fast-track by the Minister for Infrastructure.
  • 149 projects are listed in Schedule 2 of the Fast-track Approvals Act, meaning they can apply for Fast-track approval.
  • On average, it has taken 119 working days for decisions on substantive applications from when officials determine an application is complete and in-scope.
