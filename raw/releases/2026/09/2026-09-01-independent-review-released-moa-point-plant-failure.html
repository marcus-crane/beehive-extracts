Independent review released into Moa Point plant failure

  • Hon Simon Watts
Local Government

 

The Government has today released the independent Crown Review into the failure of the Moa Point Wastewater Treatment Plant and the flooding event on 4 February 2026. 

“The failure of the Moa Point Plant was one of the most significant wastewater infrastructure failures experienced in the Wellington region in recent decades," Local Government Minister Simon Watts says. 

“The public deserves answers about how such a failure occurred, and more importantly, what needs to change to prevent a similar event from happening again.”

The independent Crown Review Team examined the wider factors that may have contributed to the failure, including governance, oversight, asset management, funding, contractual arrangements and operational practices. It did not determine legal liability or identify a single technical fault. 

“Its purpose was to identify the systemic lessons that can strengthen the management and resilience of wastewater infrastructure in Wellington and across New Zealand,” Mr Watts says. 

“The review found the event was not caused by a single decision or isolated failure, but by longstanding weaknesses in governance, accountability, asset management, risk management and infrastructure resilience.  

“The review’s central finding is that preventing a similar event in the future depends on clear accountability throughout the system, supported by a strong understanding of risks and effective decision-making at every level. 

“That is a finding that extends well beyond Moa Point.” 

The Crown Review Team made recommendations for Wellington City Council and Tiaki Wai, including: 

  • restoring compliance with resource consents 
  • improving public communication about infrastructure risks 
  • strengthening governance and accountability 
  • improving asset stewardship 
  • ensuring risk management is directly linked to investment and service delivery decisions. 

Many of these improvements are already underway, with recovery and improvement works at Moa Point expected to be completed in February 2027. 

The report also included recommendations to the Minister of Local Government.

“I have accepted the recommendations directed to me and will be writing to Wellington City Council and Tiaki Wai to require a response to the report's findings. I will also work with ministerial colleagues to consider any broader regulatory and policy implications arising from the review,” Mr Watts says.

“The key challenge now is ensuring these lessons are acted on so communities can have confidence in wastewater systems that are safer, more resilient and better prepared for the future.

“The review reinforces the importance of the Government’s Local Water Done Well reforms. The findings highlight the consequences of weak accountability, poor visibility of infrastructure risks, and insufficient long-term planning and investment. 

“While no system can eliminate every infrastructure failure, Local Water Done Well establishes stronger safeguards to identify risks earlier, improve oversight, support long-term investment and hold water service providers accountable for performance. 

“As these reforms are implemented across the country, we expect to see a stronger and more resilient water sector, and a reduced likelihood of failures like Moa Point occurring in the future.”
