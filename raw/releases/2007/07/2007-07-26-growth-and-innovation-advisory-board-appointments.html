26 July 2007

Growth and Innovation Advisory Board appointments

  • Trevor Mallard
Economic Development

Economic Development Minister Trevor Mallard today announced the appointment of businessman Stephen Tindall as the new chair of the Growth and Innovation Advisory Board (GIAB).

"I am also announcing three new appointments to the board - Icebreaker chie executive Jeremy Moon, farming industry expert Sam Robinson and Business New Zealand chief executive Phil O’Reilly.

"These new appointees will bring diverse skills and expertise to the work of GIAB. The appointments follow the resignations of previous GIAB chair Ann Sherry, Peter Biggs and Theresa Gattung whom I would like to thank for their valuable contributions and hard work," Trevor Mallard said.

The board was established by the Labour-led government in May 2002 to provide the Prime Minister and senior economic ministers with advice on how the government can best promote growth and innovation in the New Zealand economy. The objective is to foster an active sector-public dialogue about growth and innovation issues.

GIAB's focus is on helping determine which policies and practices are likely to work best to support the growth of New Zealand’s business sector, within the context of an inclusive economy (given New Zealand's unique competitive positioning, our history, and our culture), and how they can be implemented, for the greatest possible benefit.

The board brokers links between government and the business sector, to progress the mutual development of strategic growth initiatives. It meets bi-monthly as a full board, feeding advice into the government's economic transformation agenda.

There are 15 board members including chairs or representatives of the boards of New Zealand Trade and Enterprise, The Foundation for Research Science and Technology and the Tertiary Education Commission, as well as leading private sector business people.

Short biographies of the new appointees follow.

Stephen Robert Tindall DCNZM, Dip. Mgt, FNZIM, HonD, Dcom Honoris Causa
Stephen Tindall has extensive business experience and is committed to community development and sustainable economic development.

He founded The Warehouse in 1982 and grew the company into a billion dollar business in both New Zealand and Australia. He held the position of managing director from 1994 until January 2001, when he was appointed founder.

Since then he has focussed on The Tindall Foundation, a charitable trust which distributes funds with the aim of helping New Zealanders to reach their full potential using the philosophy of a hand up not a hand out. The Tindall Foundation has been involved in business incubation and providing venture capital to young enterprises.

Jeremy Moon
Jeremy Moon is the CEO of merino outdoor clothing company Icebreaker, a brand launched in 1995 and now New Zealand’s largest adventure-wear exporter. Icebreaker clothing is now sold in 18 countries and has been listed on the Deloitte New Zealand ‘Fast 50’ for two years. Icebreaker also won the 2003 Design in Business award.

Jeremy Moon is also the chairman of the Better by Design Advisory Board and a member of the Business Advisory Board for the Otago University School of Business. He has a Bachelor of Commerce and Masters of Commerce in Marketing Management (specialising in consumer behaviour) from Otago University.

Samuel Robinson
Samuel Robinson has extensive experience in the farming industry and strong links with the food and beverage sector. He is a self-employed sheep and beef farmer and former chair of Richmond Ltd. He is on the Board of AgriQuality Ltd, Chair of the Agricultural and Marketing Research and Development Trust
and was a member of the Food and Beverage Taskforce.

In the private sector he is on the boards of the Port of Napier, Farmlands Trading Society Ltd the Public Service Investment Society and Centralines Ltd. Samuel Robinson holds a B Ag Sc (hons) and is an accredited fellow of the Institute of Directors.

Phil O’Reilly
Phil O'Reilly is Chief Executive of Business NZ, New Zealand's largest business advocacy organisation. He has a background in business, advocacy and communications in enterprises in New Zealand and Australia.

During the 1980s he was Industrial Advocate, Auckland Employers Association Inc. In 1990 he became Executive Director of the Newspaper Publishers Association of NZ. In 2000 he was appointed Head of Employment Policy and Communication at Westpac Bank in Sydney before returning to New Zealand to his role at Business NZ.

Phil has an MA Hons in History from Auckland University, and has attended Stanford University and the Macquarie Graduate School of Management.
