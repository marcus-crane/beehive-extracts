21 September 2006

Whangarei's Pulse shows the way for one-stop service

  • David Benson-Pope
Social Development and Employment

During a visit to the Pulse Heartlands centre today, Social Development Minister David Benson-Pope said it provided an excellent model for future 'one-stop-shop' service centres for families and young people.

The Pulse, Te Hotu Manawa, is one of only two urban Heartlands Centres in New Zealand. Operated by the Whangarei Youth One Stop Shop Charitable Trust, it provides an alternative education programme, a teen parent school, and access to youth development, employment, training, social, and health services, including alcohol and drug treatment services.

Mr Benson-Pope said that the government was increasingly keen on the one-stop model of integrated and collaborative services, and that the Pulse provided an excellent model for the Early Years service hubs that would soon be opening in high-needs communities.

"Early Years, which I announced on Monday, involves setting up service hubs to connect families to all the early intervention services available, and ensuring those services are well co-ordinated," Mr Benson-Pope said. "We're also establishing teen parent co-ordinators in areas with high rates of teenage pregnancy – and again, there will be much to learn from the Pulse's work in this area."

Mr Benson-Pope said that early intervention was about acting early in the life of a child, or early in the life of a problem. "We want to give all children the best possible start in life, and we want to support young people into healthy, positive lives as adults," he said.

"This involves a whole mix of things, including education, training, and employment, and access to the right services at the right time. The Pulse provides this support exceptionally well, and I congratulate everyone involved in this unique and highly effective service."

The Minister's trip to Northland today also involved a launch of Family Start in Kaikohe, and visits to the Trade Enterprise Programme at Northland College, and to the New World Industry Partnership.
