10 April 1998

South Pacific Private Sector Aid

  • Simon Upton
Associate Minister of Foreign Affairs and Trade

Seventeen training and business development grants to support private sector development in the Pacific Islands were announced today by Foreign Affairs Associate Minister Simon Upton.

The grants, totalling NZ$552,766, come under the Pacific Islands Investment & Development Scheme (PIIDS), part of New Zealand's Official Development Assistance (NZODA) programme. They cover private sector activities in eight countries.

"The development of a vigorous private sector is necessary to obtain a sustainable increase in living standards in the Pacific," Mr Upton said.

"With public sector budgets facing real constraints, the private sector has the capacity to generate sustainable economic growth in the Pacific region."

Mr Upton said PIIDS promoted more effective business and training links between the private sector in New Zealand and their counterparts in neighbouring South Pacific nations.

"The scheme is an innovative, forward-looking use of our ODA funding, and provides an opportunity for the New Zealand private sector be involved in assisting our neighbours to meet their own developmental needs," he said.

In the February bidding round, 32 proposals were submitted. Grants were allocated according to each proposal's development merit, commercial viability and the likely long-term economic and employment benefits for the recipient country.

Proposals for the next round of PIIDS grants should be sent to the Development Cooperation Division of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade by 3 July, 1998.


The seventeen proposals cover activities in eight South Pacific countries:

PIIDS Advisory Missions

Dave Stevenson and Joe Newson (Russell) will provide technical advice to Paerata Smallgoods (Honiara) on the production of high quality bacon, hams and smallgoods for domestic consumption.

Hort+Research (Auckland) will provide technical advice to Sogeri Spice Ltd (Port Moresby) on producing new products from excess pulp created during its pineapple juicing process.

Ross Corbett of Tourism Resource Consultants (Wellington) will assist the Kosrae Visitor Association (Micronesia) and its members to develop new and existing tourism initiatives, specifically in the areas of adventure and nature tourism.

Hort+Research (Auckland) will work with the Rarotonga Nita Growers Association to establish internationally acceptable quality standards for pawpaw exported from the Cook Islands, and to identify mechanisms for improving financial returns to growers through adding value to pawpaw that do not meet the standards required for export.

Mr Keith McRobbie (Papakura) will work with Beachcomber Ltd (Rarotonga) to train Cook Islanders in the care and maintenance of a horse and buggy which is used as a tourist attraction in the main centre of Rarotonga. The Advisory Mission will involve a 26 week training
course.

Roger Dickie (Tauranga) will assist Le Pescadou Fisheries (Vanuatu) to set up a bottom line fishing boat to harvest red snapper. The New Zealand partner will oversee all stages of preparing the boat, including alterations to deck space, insulation and painting, tuning electronic equipment, developing safety sta ndards, and overhauling the engine and electrical systems.

Texturite Coatings Ltd (Paraparaumu) will work with Tiegan Construction (Lami, Fiji) to provide specific advice on the process to achieve a 'level five' finish on Gibraltar board lined wall surfaces.

WISE (Women In Self Employment) Network (Lower Hutt) will work with self employed women in the Solomon Islands to develop a network for self-employed women to promote their businesses, share ideas, increase inter-venture trade, and keep pace with relevant regulations and opportunities.

Prue Townsend (Porirua) will train representatives from two rural villages in Fiji in hand made paper production using natural materials and fibres.

Hort+Research (Auckland) will provide technical assistance to the Friendly Island Marketing Cooperative (Nuku'alofa) on upgrading their vanilla processing facility. The Advisory Mission will focus on enhancing the vanillin content in processed vanilla beans, therefore increasing the product's value in export markets.

Alastair Robinson of the New Zealand School of Fisheries (Nelson) will provide advice and training to Solander Pacific Ltd (Fiji) to
improve navigation and safety practices for the company's fishing boat captains.

PIIDS Training Programmes

Roger Dickie (Tauranga) and the Vanuatu Fisherman's Co-operative will develop a pre-sea training programme in sea safety, boat terminology, fire fighting and rope work for young Ni-Vanuatu about to undertake employment on foreign fishing vessels.

John Stewart & Roger Dickie (Tauranga) will deliver a training programme in basic sea safety and navigation practices for private sector fishing companies on Savai'i, Samoa.

PIIDS Equity Grants

Wilson Investments Limited (Samoa) and Ace Exports Limited (Auckland) will form Samoa Coconut Products, a joint venture company to produce toiletries and soap products with a coconut oil base. The company will focus on producing cosmetic and laundry soap to match other products available on the local market.

Pezaro Farms Ltd (Albany) will form a joint venture with a local partner to produce chilled fresh and processed herbs in Fiji for export to New Zealand in winter. Over time, the venture intends to expand production to supply the local market for the tourist culinary trade, as well as export to Australia and Japan.

Steve Trued (Russell) and Ms Ake Hosea (Rarotonga) will form "Experience Sailing Limited", a joint venture company in the Cook Islands. The venture will increase the range of tourism attractions on Rarotonga, providing sea kayak lagoon tours and ocean sailing tours around Rarotonga.

Richard Cook (Samoa) and Paul Cook (Matakana) have formed "Tropical Organic Products (TOPs)", a joint venture company registered in Samoa. TOPs have developed a mulched organic coconut husk product for use as an organic medium in a wide range of horticultural applications. MOCH will marketed to the nursery and compost production sectors in New Zealand, and hydroponic gardeners in the Pacific region.

Background Note: The Pacific Islands Investment & Development Scheme (PIIDS)

The Pacific Islands Investment & Development Scheme is funded as part of the New Zealand Official Development Assistance (NZODA) programme, and is the main vehicle for delivering private sector assistance to the Pacific region.

Basic Objectives Of PIIDS

To facilitate the transfer of knowledge from New Zealand to the South Pacific through the provision of training, marketing, technical, and managerial support;
To encourage private sector development in Pacific Island Countries in partnership with the New Zealand private sector;
To create employment and economic growth in the South Pacific based on Pacific Island economies' comparative advantages;
To encourage New Zealand private sector investment in the Pacific Islands.

How the Scheme Works

The scheme aims to directly meet the needs of the private sector in the Pacific. PIIDS has been designed to be flexible with a range of funding options available including training, feasibility, and equity assistance.

There are six separate grants available under the scheme. PIIDS Development can provide grants of up to $25,000 to facilitate Training Needs Analyses, Training Programmes, Advisory Missions, and Personnel Attachments to New Zealand. PIIDS Investment allows for the funding of up to 50% of the cost of a feasibility study to a maximum of $10,000 and, provided that a proposal fits strict criteria, may contribute up to $75,000 as an Equity Grant to the new venture on a dollar for dollar basis.

PIIDS proposals are assessed by a panel made up of Development Cooperation Division staff and an independent private sector consultant during three competitive assessment rounds each year. Proposals are assessed on the basis of their developmental merit, and in the case of PIIDS Investment - their commercial viability.

The total 1997/98 PIIDS allocation is $2 million.
