1 December 2005

Health Innovation Awards finalist announced

  • Pete Hodgson
Health

A project to prevent diabetes and an initiative to link rural people to health services through video conferencing are among the 25 finalists for the 2006 Health Innovation Awards announced today by Health Minister Pete Hodgson.

"The Health Innovation Awards showcase excellence in health treatment and rehabilitation services that benefit all New Zealanders," Pete Hodgson said.

"They recognise and celebrate those working throughout the health sector to develop better products, services and processes to improve disease prevention, treatment, care and recovery. "

The awards were started in 2003 and are a joint project of the Ministry of Health and ACC with support from Telecom. This year 111 expressions of interest for the awards were received which have been narrowed to a group of 25 finalists.

The 2006 Awards have two categories: Innovation and Process Improvement. The innovation category is divided into two sections, large and small innovation, depending on the number of people involved in developing the innovation.

The process improvement category was introduced this year to respond to the large number of process improvement oriented entries received in previous years.

The finalists will now be asked to prepare a detailed application to be submitted by February for evaluation and judging. The New Zealand Business Excellence Foundation manages the independent evaluation process, using international criteria and evaluation processes.

The winners will be announced at a gala Awards Dinner ceremony and presentation in Wellington on 29 June next year.

Further information about the awards is available online at http://www.healthinnovationawards.co.nz.

Small Innovation Award Finalists:

  • Costing and Business Solutions for the design and implementation of an activity based accounting system and patient data warehouse;
  • Point Wizard for decision making processes for access to elective surgery;
  • Capital and Coast DHB for their integrated real time patient management system for intensive care;
  • Central Pharmacy for the safe and efficient disposal of unused medicines;
  • Ngati Porou Hauora Inc and Otago University for Ngati and Healthy: a population approach to preventing diabetes in the Ngati Porou rohe;
  • Capital and Coast DHB for the development of computer software for manufacturing chemotherapy heralds in the new Pharmacy Production Unit;
  • Canterbury DHB for its heart failure rehabilitation project;
  • Canterbury DHB for a system allowing target controlled delivery of volatile anaesthetics;
  • Southland DHB for a video conferencing model for rural health to connect people;
  • Wound Management Consultancy for its advanced wound care project;
  • Canterbury DHB for the SPRINT protocol for tight glycaemic control in critically ill patients;
  • West Coast DHB for their project Spring into Action - moving to a better way of health;
  • Rotorua General Practice Group for options for patient treatment;
  • Otago DHB for its integrated medical centre

Large Innovation Award Finalists:

  • East Tamaki Healthcare Services for organisational change management - making a case for change: a clinical governance tool;
  • Waitemata DHB for their beverage guidelines for schools to enable them to promote healthier drink options for children to select;
  • West Coast DHB for its Primary Integration Systems Management project - overcoming isolation through innovation;
  • Hutt Valley DHB for Lifestyle Pasifica - getting healthy the Pacific way

Process Improvement Award Finalists:

  • Auckland DHB for its improved acute medical and surgical assessment project;
  • On TRACC for its On TRACC Transculture and Intersectoral Care Centre;
  • Canterbury DHB for mental health screening in a women's prison;
  • Canterbury DHB for improving communication efficiency - Vocera paging pilot at Christchurch Hospital Emergency Department;
  • The Royal College of GPs for building quality practice systems;
  • Canterbury DHB for its blood usage project - "red blood cells - making a little go a long way";
  • Tauranga Orthopaedic Research Society for patient driven outcomes in joint replacement surgery
